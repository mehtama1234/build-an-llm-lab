A guess, with odds.

We've followed a word all the way through the model. Here's what actually comes out the other end: not a word — a probability for every one of the ~50,000 possible next tokens. Everything you've heard about “creativity,” “randomness,” and “temperature” is just what you do with that one list of odds. Let's read the real one out of GPT-2.

That number — how spread out the odds are — is the model's confidence, and it's different at every single word it writes.
Experiment B — temperature is one knob that reshapes the odds

Before picking, you can divide every score by a “temperature” T. Below 1 sharpens the odds toward the favorite; above 1 flattens them toward a coin-flip. Same model, same sentence — only the knob changes.

Experiment C — how a word actually gets picked

You still have to choose one token. The two extremes: always take the favorite (greedy), or roll the weighted dice (sampling). Watch what each does.

Greedy — always the single most likely token. Safe, and it falls into loops:

greedy · always the top token
The old house at the end of the street was a little bit of a mess. The old house was a little bit

Sampling — draw from the odds (here keeping the smallest set of top tokens that add up to 90% of the mass, so the junk tail is cut). Same prompt, three runs, three different stories:

sample 1 · rolled the dice (T=0.9, top-90%)
The old house at the end of the street was a complete wreck. We didn't know how to fix it or if
sample 2 · rolled the dice (T=0.9, top-90%)
The old house at the end of the street was the house I had moved to from Nepal a few years ago, but
sample 3 · rolled the dice (T=0.9, top-90%)
The old house at the end of the street was a very big building, even though it was completely abandoned. I remember
Greedy circles back on itself (“a little bit of a mess… a little bit”) because the safest next word, every time, tends to repeat. Sampling stays lively — but it can only stay sensible because we first threw away the long tail of bad options (that's what “top-p” / nucleus does). This is the real dial behind “make the AI more creative.”
Why this matters

Everything the model “decides” happens here

The network's job ends at one probability distribution over the vocabulary. It never “chooses” a word — you do, with a sampling rule. Confidence, creativity, repetition, randomness, “temperature” — none of it is mysterious once you see the odds: they're all just different ways of reading and trimming this one list. The same model can be a boring parrot or a wild improviser without changing a single weight.

  • The real output is odds, not a word. A full probability over ~50,000 tokens — peaked when the answer is forced (0.1 bits), flat when it's open (8.6 bits).
  • Temperature reshapes those odds before you pick — sharper (safe) or flatter (surprising), from the identical distribution.
  • The sampling rule is the personality. Greedy loops; sampling with a trimmed tail stays both varied and coherent. That choice, not the weights, is “creativity.”
Connecting it back — the end of the line
