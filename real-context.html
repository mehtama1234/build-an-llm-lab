The same word, two meanings.

Back in Session 2, every word got one fixed vector — “bank” is “bank,” always the same numbers. But “river bank” and “bank account” plainly don't mean the same thing. So how does one frozen vector become two meanings? Here we feed real GPT-2 both sentences and watch the vector for “bank” pull apart, layer by layer, into a river and a place for money.

The setup

One token, two sentences

“They dragged the canoe up onto the muddy river bank.”

“She deposited her paycheck at the local bank.”

The word “bank” enters the model as the exact same 768-number vector in both — it's one entry in the dictionary. Everything that makes it mean two different things has to be built up inside the layers, from the words around it. So we grab the “bank” vector at every one of the model's 13 stages and ask a simple question: how different are the two “bank”s here?

Why this matters

This is what “understanding context” actually is

Static word vectors (Session 2) gave every word one fixed meaning — “bank” could never tell a river from a vault. What you're watching here is the fix, and it's the whole reason transformers work: the layers rewrite each word's vector using its neighbors, so the same input word becomes a different representation depending on the company it keeps. Attention moves the neighboring information in (Exp #2); the feed-forward layers sharpen it into meaning (Exp #3); the result is a vector that means “riverside” in one sentence and “financial institution” in the next.

  • The meaning isn't in the word — it's built. “Bank” arrives identical in both sentences (0.99) and leaves as two different things (0.26), assembled purely from context.
  • It drifts the right way. Each “bank” moves toward the correct sense's neighborhood, and the gap widens with depth — meaning gets sharper the deeper you go.
  • It's general. The same splitting happens for “spring,” “bat,” and every other word whose meaning depends on context — which is most of them.
Connecting it back

Session 2 handed each word a single frozen vector. This is what the rest of the model does with it: one running vector per word, rewritten at every layer by its neighbors, until “bank” has quietly become two different words — river (0.75→ river) and money (0.83→ money) — from one identical starting point. Static meaning in; contextual meaning out.
