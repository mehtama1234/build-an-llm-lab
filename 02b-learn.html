Where do the vectors come from?

The map from the last lesson looked designed — but nobody placed those points. Every vector starts as random numbers, and a single pressure pushes them into place: predict a word from its neighbors. Below is a real tiny embedding, trained from scratch, organizing itself.

The reframe

They start as noise

An embedding table is just a block of parameters — at initialization, every token's vector is small random numbers. At that moment king and queen are nowhere near each other. Nothing about meaning is built in. So how does the structure appear?

The force

"You shall know a word by the company it keeps"

Training gives the model one job: predict a word from the words around it. To do that well, words that appear in similar contexts are pushed toward similar vectors — because a similar vector makes a similar prediction. That's the whole engine. From our corpus, the real company each word keeps:

kingkeeps company with →princethronekingcrown
queenkeeps company with →princeruledqueenthrone
catkeeps company with →catpettailpurred
threekeeps company with →countequalsonetwo

Why this creates the map

king and queen keep the same company (prince, throne, ruled) → to predict those neighbors, the model gives them similar vectors → they drift together. cat keeps different company → it drifts elsewhere. Meaning-as-geometry is a side effect of predicting from context — nobody labels "these are similar."

In a real LLM

Same force, bigger job

An LLM doesn't train embeddings separately. The embedding table is part of the model, and it's shaped by the model's one task — predict the next token — via the same gradient descent that trains everything else. A token's vector ends up wherever best helps predict what follows it. Similar-context tokens still converge; the geometry we saw is the residue of billions of next-token guesses.

Key idea

The embedding isn't a lookup someone built — it's learned weights, updated by the same signal as the rest of the network. "Meaning" is just whatever positions made prediction easier.

The one-line aha

Start with noise, reward predicting a word from its neighbors, and meaning falls out as geometry — same-company words converge because a similar vector makes a similar prediction. The map is learned, never designed.
