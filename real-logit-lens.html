Watching the guess form.

The whole lab has claimed one thing: a model doesn't compute its answer in a single shot — it builds it up, layer by layer, refining a running description of each word until it's good enough to predict what comes next. That's a nice story. This is the first experiment on a real, trained GPT-2, and it lets us actually see the guess take shape.

The question

Can we see the answer being assembled?

We said the model carries a running description of the last word up through its layers, and only at the very top reads it off as a prediction. If that's true, then the description partway up should already be leaning toward some guess — a rough, half-formed one that sharpens as it climbs. The problem is that a half-finished description is just a list of numbers; you can't read a word off it directly.

Unless — you borrow the model's own reading step. The model turns a finished description into a word-guess using one final operation. There's nothing stopping us from applying that same operation to an unfinished description, at every layer, and asking: if the model were forced to guess right now, what would it say? Do that at all 13 checkpoints and you get a frame-by-frame film of the guess forming. (People call this the logit lens.)

Read it from the bottom up and you watch the model think:

  • Layers 0–4: “George” — it just echoes the word it can see. No real prediction yet.
  • Layers 5–10: “Bush”, and increasingly sure of it. It has locked onto the most common completion of the name George ___ — a reasonable bet from the surface pattern alone.
  • Layer 11: it flips to “Washington”, hard (0.82). Somewhere in that one layer the context “first president of the United States” finally overrides the surface habit. The model changed its mind.

What this proves — not asserts

The prediction is genuinely assembled across depth, not produced at the end. And later layers don't just sharpen the early guess — they can overrule it: the model's first instinct was the common phrase “George Bush,” and a deeper layer corrected it using the sentence's meaning. That is the residual stream carrying a running “current best guess” that every layer is free to revise.

Being honest about the method

What the lens does and doesn't show

  • It's a real read, lightly approximated. The model's reading step is tuned for finished descriptions; pointing it at half-finished ones is a fair peek, not an exact transcript. The trajectories are trustworthy; treat any single mid-layer probability as rough.
  • This is the small GPT-2 (124M). It's weak, so some answers are mundane (“The capital of France is” → “the”). We picked prompts where its knowledge is solid enough to watch the mechanism clearly — the shape of the forming guess is the point, not the model's smarts.
  • Nothing here was staged. Every number is a direct read from the real weights — the Bush→Washington change of mind is what the model actually does.
Connecting it back

The capstone said the stack keeps refining a running description until it can predict, and only reads it off at the end. Here that stops being a story: the guess really is built layer by layer, it starts as filler, it passes through confident-but-wrong, and a late layer can overrule the surface habit using the meaning of the sentence. Depth isn't decoration — depth is the guess being assembled.
